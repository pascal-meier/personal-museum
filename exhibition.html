Bachelor’s Thesis

Research / Randomness in digital games / 2026

My role Research, taxonomy & system design

The question

How does the design of randomness relate to player experience in digital games?

My contribution

For my bachelor’s thesis, I studied different randomness mechanics in digital games, grouped them and brought them together in one model. Its purpose is to help game developers see what forms of randomness are available and choose mechanics for their own games.

Design decision

The model connects two questions: Where does chance come into play, and how do players interpret the outcome? Developers can use these questions while designing a mechanic to consider how it might shape the player experience.

Result & scope

In RanWorld, my playable prototype, I use selected mechanics to illustrate how the model can be applied to a game.

Full thesis title & title page

AnimalSoccer

Solo project / Unity / In development

My role Solo game development

Game concept

I developed AnimalSoccer over a weekend for a private game jam. Six animals play football one on one, each with its own ability and home arena.

My contribution

I designed the game, decided how the characters and arenas would look, and built it in Unity. I recorded the sound effects myself. Codex helped me with programming and building the characters and environments.

Design

The goal is the same for every animal: score five goals. Each animal has its own way to strike the ball. The rhino hits it with its horn, while the meerkat kicks it during a backflip.

Current status

In the prototype, you can choose a character and play career matches in several arenas. I’m still working on game feel and balance.

Pioneer of Prehistory

Team project / My graphics & assets / 2024

My role Character graphics & assets

The result

This creature is the playable character in Pioneer of Prehistory. The gallery shows it from three sides, with different fur and armour variants, and in motion.

My contribution

I drew all the sprites and animations for the character by hand and created other assets for the game. For the ten-frame walk cycle, I drew the body, arms, legs and tail on separate layers.

A design decision

Body, arms, legs and tail use a shared 64 × 64 canvas. Separate layers make the construction of the movement visible.

A character from three sides

Asset construction / Original layers

The four layers of the walk cycle

For each frame, the body, arms, legs and tail share a 64 × 64 pixel canvas. The four layers line up when the animation plays.

Keeping the parts connected

The archive groups assets by environment, movement, body part, surface variant and direction. Matching names connect each image to the same animation frame.

Esuap

Team project / React & TypeScript / 2025

My role Frontend implementation & visual identity

The task

We developed Esuap as part of our team project Cargonaut. Drivers can offer rides for passengers or transport cargo.

My contribution

I built the entire frontend in React and TypeScript and designed the logo, mock-ups, design boards and graphics. The frontend connects to our team’s GraphQL backend.

Design decision

Shared layouts, form fields and feedback connect the application’s workflows. Green and teal, light panels and the truck-shaped logo carry the same visual identity from the start screen into the journey form.

The team’s result

The interface covers accounts, vehicles, profiles and ride offers. Drivers and passengers each have their own view.
